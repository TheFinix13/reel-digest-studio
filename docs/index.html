What is this?

Reels are short videos on Instagram. The owner saves the useful-looking ones into folders. Two folders feed this system: Tech (named “Tech bro” on Instagram: videos about coding, AI tools and design) and Stocks (videos about investing, trading and money).

What does it do?

It collects new videos, writes out what is said and shown, checks the claims against trustworthy sources, decides what is worth using, and puts good ideas to work in real projects. No person has to approve each one.

How to read the page

The map shows the six stages. Click a stage to see what happens there. Further down you can follow one video from start to finish, see what we kept and why, and check how far the system can be trusted.

Words used on this page
Reel
A short video on Instagram.
Claim
A statement in a video that could be true or false, such as “this tool is free” or “this bot made $55,000”.
Source
A trustworthy web page, such as an official site or a public code project, that confirms or contradicts a claim.
Sales funnel
A post built to make you comment or message so that someone can sell to you. These are treated as untrustworthy.
Use now / Test first
“Use now” means the idea is confirmed and can be used. “Test first” means it looks real but should be tried safely on a small scale before relying on it.
Second AI
An AI from a different company that double-checks decisions. Two similar AIs tend to agree with each other, so a different one is used on purpose.
Agent
A program or AI that does one job. The agents here are listed under each stage.
Claude, Cursor, Raphael
Claude is an AI assistant that reads the folders. Cursor is an AI coding assistant that applies changes. Raphael is the owner’s personal AI assistant.
Confidence band
On the reliability bars, the coloured band shows where the true result very likely sits (95% sure). A wide band means there were too few examples to be confident.
